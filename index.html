Technical Report
ENG 2003

Term Project 1 - "Certifying AI-Embedded Robotic Systems as Safe"

Formal technical report, 14 pages · ENG 2003, Summer 2026

Why I selected this piece
It is the most complete document I have ever written. Letter of transmittal, executive summary, table of contents, list of figures and tables, five numbered sections, and thirteen IEEE references. The topic is a real and unsolved problem: robots are moving out of their cages and into shared workspaces under Industry 5.0, many of them now run on AI, and there is no safety certification standard built for that. I picked it because I wanted a subject where I had to convince the reader of something, not just inform them of the issue.
What it says about my development
The clearest evidence is a structural decision I made against advice that an AI gave me. I was given a layout that split everything into a broad "Results and Discussion" block with sub-sections for perception, decision checking, physical limits, and what is still missing. I simply rejected it and instead used my own layout. I made that call because it reads more easily for a person going through the entire report from start to finish. I've also documented the suggestions and disagreement within the report rather than quietly following the suggestions. Having the section for AI use made me more aware of how I use AI and caused me to depend less on AI.
Did it push me out of my comfort zone?
Yes, mainly because of the subject. I am a hardware and low-level software person, and this report required me to read international safety standards (ISO 10218-1/2, ISO/TS 15066, IEC 61508) and Canadian AI legislation (AIDA, under the Bill C-27 that never passed) and then write about them accurately. Arguing a position I could not settle by building the entire solution as a project to the problem was uncomfortable because I started being more passionate about the subject and wanted to make a real change.
Is this my best work?
This is my most complete work, but the process was not my best, because I did not hand in Phase 2, causing me to not get an alternate perspective on how to write the report nor try to see if the layout I've made myself was clear and understandable for a reader, making the final report appear as a first draft instead of a revised version.
Cover Letter
ENG 2003

Cover Letter - Associate Developer at IBM

Professional correspondence · ENG 2003 Assignment 1, Summer 2026

Why I selected this piece
This is the shortest assignment I've written, but the one that makes each word have a deliberate meaning behind it. It is one page long, aimed at a hiring manager who has no interest in me and would also stop reading the second they feel that I am not interesting or useful for the position.
What it says about my development
It made me more aware of how to fit as much impactful information with a word count limit, because of that I stopped listing what I've done and instead talked about what I've produced since it has greater meaning behind it, and use it to impress the reader instead of boring the reader. I believe I've written one of my best lines in the assignment about the automated outlier tool for filling machine data and how it saved the company upwards of $1.5 million a year. I've also split the professional experience and personal projects into separate paragraphs. In my previous cover letter before this course, the same experience was all combined together with no clear layout.
Did it push me out of my comfort zone?
It did push me out of my comfort zone, because I am comfortable explaining my projects and how they work at great lengths, but having to argue that I am worth hiring is very difficult for me and also having to meet the word count limit made it more uncomfortable.
Is this my best work?
This is the most concise work I've done, but not my best work. I can improve the introduction. Saying that I am drawn to IBM because of their talent and the scale of the problems is something I could write about any company, and the hiring manager has definitely read that sentence before. If I were to redo this assignment, I would change the introduction around something only IBM does. I am proud of the other paragraphs, and I would consider them good work done by me.
Handout Brochures
ENG 2003

Handout - The Same Research, Written for Two Audiences

Two tri-fold brochures · ENG 2003 Assignment 3, Summer 2026

Why I selected this piece
It is the same research as my technical report, rewritten twice for two completely different audiences, and putting the two side by side shows the difference. One brochure is called "Robots and Humans: Better Together!" and is aimed at a high school student reader. The other is called "Certifying AI-Embedded Robots as Safe" and is aimed at someone more technical and familiar with the industry.
What it says about my development
The brochure for high school students is simpler and talks about the problem using self-checkouts, robotic arms and delivery drones, then breaks the robot safety into 3 different layers that a non-engineer can easily understand. The technical brochure talks about the same problem but differently, putting deterministic robots against probabilistic outputs from AI robots then names the 3 certification barriers. Both versions aren't dumbed down, but instead each brochure uses different evidence for different readers.
Did it push me out of my comfort zone?
Yes, because making the technical brochure was easier than making the high school brochure without removing any importance or by quietly making the claim wrong and hoping the reader would not catch on. Keeping every simplified sentence accurate is very difficult.
Is this my best work?
The visual work is what I am proud of and how I've kept the layout and sections consistent on both brochures, but the section panel density is uneven. The reference sections are packed tightly and are very hard to read without zooming in whilst the "Can We Trust Robots With Our Safety?" section has unused space.
Project Documentation
Outside ENG 2003

Documentation for my 16-bit Computer

Personal project write-up · 2025-2026 · github.com/GoravM/16-bit-Computer

Why I selected this piece
I built a working 16-bit computer in HDL that executes assembly, and for a while the only person who could understand it was me. The markdown file I wrote to fix that is the piece of communication I am proudest of, because nobody assigned it and nobody was going to grade it. It is also the honest test of whether this project stuck to me and also if I can effectively communicate the idea as well.
What it says about my development
Writing it on Github meant that I was writing for a reader who I'll never meet, and who might not even exist. It was all just out of my own decision to explain everything about the project. From the simple Elementary Logic Gates to the Arithmetic Logic Unit, Memory System, to the Computer itself and how it works then to the Assembler and then the VM Translator.
Did it push me out of my comfort zone?
It did push me out of my comfort zone, because there is no rubric or even an audience, so however much I've detailed for the reader is based on my own judgement. Deciding what to not explain and what to explain was hard without having to rely on a marking scheme.
Is this my best work?
The engineering is the best I've done. I've put a lot of effort into communicating the idea, and also showing that I have an idea of exactly everything within the project. Despite me being very proud of it, there is still more to work on such as explaining the VM translator, then making a language similar to Java, and afterwards making my own Operating Software.

Section 2

Reflection

Part I - My experience in ENG 2003

My overall experience.

My overall experience in the course was good, and it was more useful than what I had expected it to be. When starting the course I treated it as just a mandatory course that I had to do, but instead it became a course that changed how I approach and write any piece of document in the future and it has also taught me more about communicating effectively, and it will definitely help me in the future.

Was the course what I expected?

No. I expected to be marked on formatting and how to write cleaner documents, but instead it taught me how to be more effective, make the reader the subject, and because of the tutorials and assignments it helped me learn how to communicate my ideas more easily to others, even if they are part of a different engineering discipline than me.

The content I found most useful / interesting.

I found making the Assignment 3 handout most useful and interesting because it taught me about how to search for and use official documents/research papers, how to find said documents/papers, how to express my ideas to different audiences, and more. Each assignment, despite being similar, was still very different, and each taught me different things. Assignment 3 and Term Project 1 were both about the same topic, but they both taught me to format and write specifically for different audiences and because of this it taught me effective engineering communication skills.

Part II - My growth as a communicator

My abilities before ENG 2003.

I would have called myself good at communicating because of my 16-bit-Computer project's documentation and how I kept the reader in mind and how I wrote it not only for myself but for others to understand what I have done.

My abilities after ENG 2003.

After taking the course and learning effective engineering communication, I will start from the reader and who they are intended to be whether they are a high school student, an expert, or anything else. So I can trust them to know what I leave out and structure it keeping the intended reader's skills in mind.

The skills I improved the most.

I have improved on report writing, evaluating the type of audience my reports are for, and communicating my ideas to others.

The skills I still need to work on.

The three skills I need to work on are visual communication — I believe I could have done Assignment 3 better visually, the picture for the ALU in my project could have been done better and simpler. Next is my speaking skill. For Assignment 2, in the interview, I had a hard time giving a clear and concise answer to each interview question, and I would take multiple tries to get it right. The final skill I need to work on is discipline, because I did not hand in Phase 2 for Term Project 1 and handed in my final draft with no revisions, and I believe if I want to be a better engineer I should have better discipline and hand in all my work, not only for marks but just to get a better reflection on what I need to work on personally.

How this changes my other engineering courses and practices.

Using what I've learned when writing my Term Project 1, I concluded that AI-driven robots cannot be certified as safe unless engineers can audit the AI on why it made a decision, as a safety requirement. The same should hold for me about how a design or paper that cannot be understood can't be reviewed, and since it's a decision I made myself, understood by no one, it cannot be checked. Communicating clearly isn't a soft skill; it is what makes the engineering work verifiable.

Beyond the Classroom

Most of what I know about explaining technical work, I learned by building things nobody asked me to build and then trying to describe them to someone else.

The project I keep coming back to

HDL · Assembly · C

16-bit computer, built from NAND gates

Personal project · github.com/GoravM/16-bit-Computer

I built a working 16-bit computer in HDL, starting from Elementary Logic Gates and using it to make the Arithmetic Logic Unit and the Memory System and combining it all into the CPU's Architecture then finally the 16-bit-Computer. I've also made the assembly language it works off of and the VM Translator. It has taught me a lot about the assembly language and how it works, which previously I couldn't understand, about how layered systems all fit together, and also about how computers work in general, and I have used what I've learned when troubleshooting since.

The engineering part was easy once I got the ALU to work, but the hard part was writing the documentation that lets others understand a system from the ground up and some of the decisions I've made, for example making comment lines, flags and whitespaces in the assembly language get translated into "0000000000000000" in the code, without expecting the reader to understand it at a first glance but instead holding their hand and explaining everything they need to know.

Transferable skill: explaining an entire system to a reader from the ground up.
